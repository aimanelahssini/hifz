Learn a surah by heart, one ayah at a time.

Hifz is a small prompter in the corner of your screen. The ayah you're on is in white, the next ones wait in grey, and Space slides to the next one. Turn the sound on and it's recited with you.

Try it:

See it in a minute and a half

From the terminal to the last ayah of Al-Wâqi'a: pick a surah, read with Space, turn on the recitation, speed it up, close it, open it again where you left off, and finish.

Small, quiet, and always there

Everything you need to memorise, nothing in the way of what you're doing.

One ayah at a time

The current ayah stays at the top in white. Space slides to the next one, Backspace goes back.

Always on top

In the top-right corner, above your windows, on every desktop, until you close it.

A recitation that follows you

Turn the sound on and the ayah at the top is recited. Muted by default, with no sound stream at all.

0.5× to 2×

← and → change the speed by 0.05, shown next to the speaker and kept for next time. The voice keeps its pitch.

Picks up where you left off

Close it any time. Next time it opens on the same surah, at the same ayah.

Counts your rounds

At the last ayah it asks if you finished. Yes adds one to the counter in the corner.

Type quran, pick a number

The terminal lists the 114 surahs. Type a number (or press Enter for the last one) and Hifz opens in the corner. Close the terminal: Hifz stays.

quran 56 and quran waqia work too, and clicking the surah's name on the card opens the list of all 114: finished ones in green, and a star to save the ones you're working on.

A smooth slide, not a jump

No word-by-word highlighting to chase. Each press of Space slides the whole text up by one ayah, so your eyes always come back to the same place.

The card keeps the same size for every surah. Long ayahs wrap and scroll inside it.

Right-click for the rest

Speed, reciter (Mishary Alafasy, Al-Husary, Al-Minshawi, Abdul Basit, As-Sudais, Al-Muaiqly, Ash-Shuraim, Ad-Dosari), continue automatically, the Arabic under each line, and back to the beginning.

Finished? Count it.

At the last ayah a question appears over the blurred text. Yes adds one to the small counter and takes you back to the start for the next round.

Every round is kept with its date. quran stats shows them all.

Phonetics written for French readers

Generated from the verified Tanzil text and written the way it's recited: the article assimilated, letters merged where the reciter merges them, pausal endings at the end of each ayah.

Install

For GNOME 50 on Wayland (made on Fedora 44). The installer adds GTK 4, libadwaita and GStreamer if they're missing.

git clone https://github.com/aimanelahssini/hifz
cd hifz
./install.sh
1 · Log out and back inonce, so GNOME loads the little extension that keeps Hifz in the corner.
2 · Type quranin a terminal and pick a surah. Al-Wâqi'a the first time.
3 · Click the card, press Spaceand the next ayah slides up. M turns the recitation on.

Questions

Does it work offline?

The text is in the app, so reading always works. The recitation is downloaded the first time you play each ayah and kept, so a surah you've listened to once works offline afterwards.

Does it work on other desktops than GNOME?

Hifz is a GTK 4 app, so it opens anywhere GTK runs, as a normal window. Keeping it on top in the corner and on every desktop is done by a small GNOME Shell extension.

Is the phonetic text exact?

It follows the recitation closely (assimilation, idghâm, iqlâb, pausal forms), but no transliteration replaces the Arabic. Press A to show the Arabic under each line, and check with a teacher.

Where is my progress kept?

On your computer only, in ~/.local/state/hifz/progress.json: the ayah you're at in each surah, how many times you finished it and when, and your settings. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Is it free?

Yes. Hifz is open source under the MIT license. The Quran text is the Tanzil Uthmani text (CC BY 3.0) and the recitations come from everyayah.com.
